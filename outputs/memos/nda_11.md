## Escalations (a lawyer decides)

**6. Permitted disclosure to representatives** (section 4). Representatives are limited to employees. Affiliates, contractors and legal, accounting and financial advisors need the Discloser's prior written consent, which may amount to a prohibition on advisor disclosure. This is ambiguous and close to an escalate trigger, so a lawyer should decide.
> The Recipient shall not otherwise disclose Confidential Information to any third party without the Discloser's prior written consent.

## Fallbacks (playbook wording to propose)

**2. Definition of Confidential Information** (section 1.1). The definition requires marking with no reasonable-person standard, which the fallback fixes. Oral and visual disclosures are covered if identified at the time, with no written summary requirement, and no escalate trigger applies. The NDA uses Discloser/Recipient in place of Disclosing Party/Receiving Party; the lawyer should conform the terms.
> in any form, that is clearly marked "Confidential" at the time of disclosure.

Propose: "Confidential Information also includes information that is not marked but that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of disclosure."
