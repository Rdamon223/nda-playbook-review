## Escalations (a lawyer decides)

**4. Term of the agreement and survival of obligations** (section 8). Obligations for all Confidential Information are perpetual, not only trade secrets, which is an escalate trigger.
> The Recipient's obligations with respect to all Confidential Information continue in perpetuity and survive any expiration or termination of this Agreement.

## Fallbacks (playbook wording to propose)

**8. Return or destruction of materials** (section 7). The 30-day period and certification are fine, but there is no carve-out for backups or legal retention. No escalate trigger applies because the NDA does not reject the fallback wording.
> Within thirty (30) days after the Discloser's written request, the Recipient shall return or destroy all of the Discloser's Confidential Information, including all copies, extracts, and summaries,...

Propose: "The Receiving Party is not required to delete Confidential Information held in automatic electronic backup systems or retained to comply with applicable law or bona fide document retention policies, provided that any retained Confidential Information remains subject to this Agreement for as long as it is retained."

**14. No license, no warranty, no obligation to proceed** (section 10). The no-license and no-obligation statements are present, but the "as is" no-warranty statement is missing. No escalate trigger applies.
> No license under any patent, copyright, trade secret, or other intellectual property right is granted or implied by this Agreement. Neither party is obligated to enter into any further agreement or...

Propose: "No license under any patent, copyright, trade secret, or other intellectual property right is granted by this Agreement. All Confidential Information is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind as to its accuracy or completeness. Neither party is obligated to enter into any further agreement or transaction, and either party may end discussions at any time."

- **3. Standard exclusions** (section 5): All four standard exclusions are present, the Recipient bears the burden of showing them with written records (acceptable), and the public-domain exclusion excludes breach by the Recipient.
- **7. Compelled disclosure** (section 6): Includes notice where permitted, cooperation at the Discloser's expense, and the only-portion-required limit.
- **2. Definition of Confidential Information** (section 1.1): The definition covers non-public business, technical and financial information in any form, uses a reasonable-person standard, covers oral disclosures with no written summary requirement, and has no carve-outs.
- **5. Permitted use (purpose limitation)** (section 3): Use is limited to the Purpose, defined in 1.2 as evaluating, negotiating and performing a services relationship, which matches the preferred position.
- **6. Permitted disclosure to representatives** (section 4): Matches the preferred position: need-to-know, bound by equivalent obligations, and the Recipient is responsible for Representatives' breaches.
- **11. Remedies and injunctive relief** (section 9): Mutual equitable relief without proof of damages, and each party bears its own fees. There are no liquidated damages or indemnities.
- **12. Governing law and venue** (section 11.2): Delaware law with exclusive Delaware venue matches the preferred position, with no arbitration or one-sided waiver.
- **13. Assignment and change of control** (section 11.1): Mutual consent requirement with the affiliate and successor exception on notice, and the agreement binds permitted successors, as the preferred position requires.
- **9. Residuals clause** (no clause): No residuals clause appears anywhere in the NDA, which is the preferred position.
